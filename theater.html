Theater

Grab a seat! This is where the site plays videos and sounds, and shows pictures in clever ways. Every screen here uses a different HTML tag.

Now showing on Pokémon TV

This video comes from the official Pokémon Kids TV channel. An <iframe> puts a window to another website inside ours.

Video not playing? Watch it on YouTube (opens in a new tab).

Bulbasaur’s sunlight lesson

Grass-type Pokémon get their energy from the sun, just like real plants. Venusaur’s giant flower even opens up to catch more sunlight. Press play to watch a real flower do the same thing.

This one uses the <video> tag. It has two <source> files so every browser can find one it knows, and a <track> with captions. Turn them on with the CC button.

Flower video: public domain (CC0), from the MDN Web Docs examples.

The cry lab

Every Pokémon makes its own sound, called a cry. These are the original cries from the very first games. Each player is an <audio> tag.

Tip: some browsers, like older Safari, can’t play .ogg sound files. Try a different browser if a cry stays quiet.

A picture that changes

Make your browser window narrow and watch this Charizard. On small screens it switches to the old pixel sprite from 1996!

The <picture> tag holds several <source> images, each with a media condition. The browser picks the first one that matches and falls back to the <img>.

Pikachu up close

Click Pikachu’s ears, cheeks, paws or tail. This is an image map: <area> tags turn parts of a picture into links.

  • Ears Pikachu’s long ears have black tips. It uses them to listen for danger and to show how it’s feeling.
  • Cheeks The red circles are electric pouches. Pikachu stores electricity in them and lets it out as sparks.
  • Paws Pikachu’s tiny paws are great for holding berries. It even zaps hard berries with electricity to make them soft enough to eat.
  • Tail Shaped like a lightning bolt! Pikachu raises it to check its surroundings, and it sometimes gets struck by lightning.
